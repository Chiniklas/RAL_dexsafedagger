Reviewer 4 of IROS 2026 submission 3013

Comments to the author
======================

The paper proposes DexSafeDagger, a policy distillation
pipeline that trains an RGB-based student from a
state-based teacher, using safe intervention triggered by
Q-value prediction and action disagreement. The method is
validated on a lift object task in simulation, with limited
hardware experiments. 

Strengths:
	- The paper is well written - the language is clear
and the notation is clear. 
	- The ablations in the simulation are clearly
explained and designed. 

Weaknesses:
	- The related work is limited and that makes it
difficult to judge the novelty of the method. There has
been many related work in safedagger extensions
(https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08364,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.00638,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14868). There has been a lot of
work in failure prediction and teacher intervention
(https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04334,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.08558). 
	- The novelty of the method is low. Every component
is a standard: asymmetric actor-critic for teacher policy,
risk predictor using SARSA Q-value, intervention mechanism
is a heuristic check with hyperparameters, student
distillation is standard MSE. The claimed contribution of
applying these methods to a new application is relatively
weak. 
	- The experiments are not sufficiently impressive:
The lift task is a good toy example, but it is not
impressive to demonstrate the method's practical utility or
scaling or performance compared to state-of-the-art. In the
simulation experiment, the proposed method
does not always outperform safedagger (fig 3). In the
hardware experiment, there are not sufficient explanation
of the experiment and there is no testing of baseline
methods. 

Minor:
	- There is a typo in algorithm and equation (6).
They use d_t (which is termination flag) instead of
\delta_t (which is disagreement)

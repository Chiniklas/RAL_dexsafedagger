Reviewer 1 of IROS 2026 submission 3013

Comments to the author
======================

(summary)
This paper proposes a safety-aware teacher–student
distillation framework that reduces unsafe exploration. It
detects risky states using both a disagreement signal and a
learned risk score, and selectively triggers teacher
intervention to guide the student policy during training.

(strengths)
Conventional DAgger-based methods typically rely on action
disagreement between the expert and the learner to trigger
intervention. In contrast, this paper introduces a Q-based
risk predictor that explicitly models state–action risk as
a separate signal. This enables intervention decisions that
consider actual failure likelihood beyond simple policy
mismatch. Furthermore, the proposed method is validated in
both simulation and real robot settings, demonstrating
effective reduction of unsafe behavior across diverse
failure modes.

(weaknesses)
First, the framework relies on a teacher policy that may
not be robust under out-of-distribution states, raising
concerns about its ability to provide reliable recovery
during intervention.
Second, the use of different observation modalities between
teacher and student is not sufficiently justified, and the
impact of this design choice on distillation performance is
not clearly analyzed.
Third, the method requires a warm-start dataset to train
the risk predictor and calibrate thresholds, which limits
its practicality, especially in safety-critical real-world
settings where collecting unsafe data is undesirable.
Fourth, it is unclear whether the performance improvement
comes from the proposed intervention mechanism itself or
simply from increased supervision due to more frequent
expert queries. A controlled comparison with matched
intervention rates would be necessary to isolate the true
contribution of the method.

Comments on the Video Attachment
================================

The video provides sufficient value by presenting both
simulation and real robot experiments. However, it is
unclear from the video how unsafe situations are detected
and how the behavior is corrected, which is a limitation.

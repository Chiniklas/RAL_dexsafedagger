The revised manuscript is substantially stronger. It now isolates
visual and semantic VLM contributions, compares fixed and adaptive
intervention strategies, reports critic detection errors, expands
real-robot trials across all 22 objects, and appropriately narrows the
safety claims. These revisions address most major concerns. Remaining
issues are primarily interpretive and statistical: unseen-object
unsafe-event rates remain high, the real-world benefit over the non-VLM
intervention baseline is modest, critic false negatives persist, and
VLM dependence introduces reproducibility and latency concerns. These
points can be handled through clarification and limited additional
analysis.


Comments

1. The revision appropriately removes formal 'safety-critical' claims.
Please maintain this discipline throughout the manuscript and
consistently describe the method as empirical risk reduction under
task-specific unsafe-event definitions.

2. Real-world unsafe-event rates on unseen objects remain around 39%,
which is substantial. This limitation should be emphasized prominently
in the abstract, discussion, and conclusion to avoid implying
deployment-ready safety.

3. The real-robot difference between VLM-guided and fixed-threshold
critic policies appears modest. Report confidence intervals and paired
or proportion tests to determine whether the observed unsafe-rate
reductions are statistically distinguishable.

4. The Bellman critic achieves a 5.8% false-negative rate on unsafe
episodes. Please discuss the consequences of these missed warnings and
whether threshold adjustment can trade task success for lower
false-negative risk.

5. Clarify whether critic metrics are computed at the episode or
time-step level and how early an unsafe event is detected before
termination. Warning lead time is important for intervention
usefulness.

6. The teacher policy is implicitly treated as a safe fallback, yet
teacher interventions can still generate unsafe contacts. Report
teacher unsafe-event rates and explain what happens when teacher and
critic recommendations conflict.

7. Provide the final exact VLM identifier, prompt, decoding settings,
visual-sample selection rules, update interval, latency distribution,
failure handling, and cost in the main paper or permanent supplementary
material.

8. The ablations convincingly motivate visual-semantic adaptation on
representative training objects. Please state more explicitly whether
the VLM adaptation itself was evaluated on novel scene semantics,
rather than only novel object geometries.

9. The current unsafe-event taxonomy is task-specific. Clarify which
events are directly sensor-observable on hardware and which depend on
privileged simulation signals during training or evaluation.

10. Please add a compact limitations paragraph covering the remaining
sim-to-real contact-strategy mismatch, external VLM dependence,
task-specific safety definitions, and absence of formal constraint
guarantees. No major methodological restructuring appears necessary.

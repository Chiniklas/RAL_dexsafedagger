This paper proposes VLM-DexSafeDagger, a safety-aware online
skill-distillation framework for transferring privileged RL teacher
policies to an RGB-based student policy for dexterous manipulation. The
method combines teacher�student action disagreement with a Bellman
success-value critic and uses a VLM to adapt the intervention
thresholds during training. Reducing unsafe exploration during
interactive skill distillation is an important problem, especially for
contact-rich dexterous manipulation. The paper is well written overall,
and the simulation results show promising reductions in unsafe episodes
while maintaining similar task success.

However, I have two main concerns: the contribution and necessity of
the VLM are not sufficiently demonstrated, and some details regarding
reproducibility and safety characterization are still missing.

1. The main concern about the proposed method is whether the
improvement comes from VLM-based reasoning or simply from adaptive
threshold tuning. The VLM uses rollout statistics and images, but its
output only adjusts two intervention thresholds. The current comparison
between DexSafeDagger and VLM-DexSafeDagger compares fixed thresholds
with VLM-adapted thresholds. Therefore, it does not clearly show
whether the improvement comes from threshold adaptation, language
reasoning, or visual information. To clarify the VLM contribution, it
would be helpful to compare cases with and without LLM/VLM, and with
and without statistics and/or images. These comparisons would help show
whether the VLM and visual information provide a clear advantage over
simpler adaptive approaches.

The related work on adaptive intervention should also be expanded. In
particular, AIM (Cai et al., ICML 2025), EnsembleDAgger, and HG-DAgger
are relevant. Since adaptive intervention can be achieved without a
VLM, the authors should clarify how VLM-DexSafeDagger differs from
these methods and whether the VLM provides an advantage over simpler
adaptive approaches.

2. Some details are still missing for reproducibility. Since the VLM
directly changes the intervention thresholds, the authors should
provide the VLM prompt and explain how the images are selected and how
the VLM outputs are used. The Bellman success-value critic is also
important for intervention, but its performance is not evaluated in
detail. Some evaluation of the critic, especially its errors for unsafe
cases, would be helpful. Please also clarify the number of runs/random
seeds used for the results. Finally, I am not fully convinced by the
use of the term �safety-critical.� The current safety evaluation is
based on task-specific unsafe conditions rather than formal safety
guarantees. The authors should clarify the scope of this claim.

Additionally, the real-world experiments are valuable and show that the
proposed method can work on physical hardware by comparing it with the
baseline system. I understand that running additional real-world
baseline experiments can be costly and difficult. However, only
VLM-DexSafeDagger evaluated on the real robot does not sufficiently
prove the proposed concept.  

Minor comments: The paper also states that 22 objects were evaluated,
while Table IV shows only six representative objects. It would be
helpful to provide overall results for all 22 objects, for example by
reporting results separately for seen and unseen objects.

three-dimensional linear and angular velocity. Running mean/variance
normalization is applied to both inputs and to the value targets.

\subsection{Termination Conditions}
We define four unsafe conditions that serve both as termination criteria during teacher-policy training and as unsafe-event signals during student-policy distillation. We additionally terminate an episode when it reaches the maximum time horizon; however, a timeout is not classified as an unsafe event. All five termination conditions are checked at \(60\,\text{Hz}\) in the environment frame.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt]
  \item \emph{Harmful collision:} We monitor all arm--table contacts and
  trigger this termination when the palm or any fingertip is less than
  1\,cm above the tabletop, or when any arm link contacts the table. An arm--table contact
  is registered when the $\ell_1$ norm of the filtered contact-force vector
  exceeds 0.1 N.
  \item \emph{Object escape:} We define a permissible object workspace above
  the table that covers the intended manipulation region.  We trigger
  object-escape termination as soon as the object center leaves this space or
  falls below the table.
  \item \emph{Palm flipped:} The palm should face downward toward the object and
  table.  We trigger this termination when its downward direction differs from
  world-down by more than $90^\circ$, at which point the nominal grasp geometry
  is inverted.
  \item \emph{Hand too far:} We use the same permissible workspace as in (b)
  and trigger this termination when the palm link leaves it, indicating that
  the hand has moved too far during exploration.
  \item \emph{Timeout:} We terminate the episode when the time index reaches
  the maximum episode horizon, $t\geq T_{\max}$.
\end{enumerate}
Expanding the termination rule used in the main paper gives
\[
\mathbb I_{\rm term}(s_t,t)
=\mathbb I_{\rm coll}(s_t)\lor\mathbb I_{\rm obj}(s_t)
 \lor\mathbb I_{\rm flip}(s_t)\lor\mathbb I_{\rm hf}(s_t)
 \lor\mathbb I_{\rm timeout}.
\]
To avoid conflicts between simultaneous termination conditions, we assign the
event label using the following descending severity priority:
$\text{harmful collision}\succ\text{object escape}\succ\text{palm flipped}
\succ\text{hand too far}\succ\text{timeout}$.

\begin{samepage}
\subsection{Output Processing and Threshold Updates}
The requested machine-readable output is a top-level JSON object with the
following schema:
\begin{verbatim}
{
  "delta_l2_threshold": <number>, "delta_success_threshold": <number>,
  "confidence": <number>, "reason": <string>
}
\end{verbatim}
\end{samepage}
The two returned values are threshold displacements. For each channel
$x\in\{\delta,Q\}$, the candidate threshold is
\[
\tilde\tau_x^{(k)}=\tau_x^{(k)}+\Delta\tau_x^{(k)}.
\]
Each candidate is clipped to a fixed range around its initial value and then
exponentially smoothed with the previously applied threshold:
\[
\bar\tau_x^{(k)}=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(
\tilde\tau_x^{(k)},c_x^{\min}\tau_x^{(0)},
c_x^{\max}\tau_x^{(0)}\right),\qquad
\tau_x^{(k+1)}=(1-\alpha)\tau_x^{(k)}+\alpha\bar\tau_x^{(k)}.
\]
We use $\alpha=0.1$ and $(c_x^{\min},c_x^{\max})=(0.5,1.5)$ for both
thresholds. The updated values are applied from the next control step through
the intervention gate
$\lVert a_t^S-a_t^T\rVert_2>\tau_\delta\ \lor
\hat Q_{\mathrm{succ}}(s_t,a_t^S)<\tau_Q$; lowering $\tau_\delta$ or raising
$\tau_Q$ increases teacher intervention. The \texttt{confidence} and
\texttt{reason} fields are logged for diagnostics and do not alter the update.
This adaptation is used only during simulation distillation; physical
deployment uses the frozen RGB student alone.

If a VLM request fails or its output cannot be parsed, the controller retains
the previously applied thresholds and tries again at the next scheduled update.
None of the reported trials encountered a condition that activated this
fallback.

\subsection{Bellman Critic Evaluation}
We use the resulting student and fixed-threshold Bellman critic from
ablation~(e) to evaluate the critic's performance. Both networks are frozen
during evaluation, and the critic runs in parallel as a passive evaluator. We
evaluate 200 episodes for each of the 40 training objects in simulation.
Table~\ref{tab:bellman_critic_evaluation} reports the observed lift-success and
unsafe-episode rates together with the task-outcome prediction accuracy, the
unsafe-episode detection rate, and its corresponding false-negative rate.

The lift-success rate and unsafe-episode rate characterize the student policy's
ability to accomplish the task and the frequency of unsafe outcomes,
respectively. Task-outcome prediction accuracy is the percentage of episodes
for which the critic correctly predicts the task outcome. The unsafe-episode
detection rate is the percentage of unsafely terminated episodes that are
flagged by the critic before termination. The unsafe false-negative rate is the
complementary percentage of unsafe episodes that the critic does not flag before
termination.

\subsection{Implementation Details of Ablation~(h)}
Ablation~(h) replaces the VLM tuner in ablation~(f) with a deterministic
performance-triggered threshold curriculum inspired by the budget-aware
intervention principle of ThriftyDAgger. The teacher, student, Bellman critic,
intervention gate, admissible threshold range, rollout budget, and update
schedule are otherwise identical to those in ablation~(f); the curriculum uses
neither language nor visual inputs.

At scheduled update~$k$, let $\mathcal W_k$ denote the most recent rollout
window. We compute its lift-success and unsafe-episode rates as
\begin{equation}
\bar s_k=\frac{1}{|\mathcal W_k|}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal W_k}
\mathbb I_{\mathrm{succ}}(\tau),\qquad
\bar u_k=\frac{1}{|\mathcal W_k|}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal W_k}
\mathbb I_{\mathrm{unsafe}}(\tau).
\label{eq:curriculum_window_metrics}
\end{equation}
The curriculum contains five ordered operating points, indexed from the most
conservative ($j=0$) to the most autonomous ($j=4$):
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
j                         & 0    & 1    & 2    & 3    & 4 \\ \hline
c_{\delta}^{(j)}           & 0.50 & 0.75 & 1.00 & 1.25 & 1.50 \\
c_{Q}^{(j)}                & 1.50 & 1.25 & 1.00 & 0.75 & 0.50
\end{array}
\label{eq:curriculum_levels}
\end{equation}
At level~$j_k$, the intervention thresholds are
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\delta}^{(k)}=c_{\delta}^{(j_k)}\tau_{\delta}^{(0)},
\qquad
\tau_{Q}^{(k)}=c_{Q}^{(j_k)}\tau_{Q}^{(0)}.
\label{eq:curriculum_thresholds}
\end{equation}
Thus, moving toward a smaller level lowers the disagreement threshold and
raises the minimum acceptable predicted-success threshold, producing more
teacher intervention. Moving toward a larger level has the opposite effect and
grants the student more autonomy. The resulting gate remains an OR rule:
intervention occurs when either
$\lVert a_t^S-a_t^T\rVert_2>\tau_{\delta}^{(k)}$ or
$\hat Q_{\mathrm{succ}}(s_t,a_t^S)<\tau_Q^{(k)}$.

Let $u_{\max}$ be a validation-selected unsafe-rate limit and $s_{\min}$ a
validation-selected competence threshold. Define
$A_k=\mathbb I[\bar u_k\leq u_{\max}\land\bar s_k\geq s_{\min}]$.
Starting from the nominal operating point $j_0=2$, the curriculum update is
\begin{equation}
j_{k+1}=
\begin{cases}
\max(j_k-1,0), & \bar u_k>u_{\max},\\
\min(j_k+1,4), & A_k=1\ \land\ A_{k-1}=1,\\
j_k,           & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:curriculum_update}
\end{equation}
Consequently, one unsafe window immediately moves the gate one level toward
more teacher intervention, whereas two consecutive windows satisfying both
safety and task-performance criteria are required before increasing student
autonomy. This asymmetric update prevents rapid oscillation. We apply the first
curriculum update after 2{,}000 distillation episodes and subsequent updates
every 10{,}000 episodes, matching the VLM update schedule in ablation~(f).

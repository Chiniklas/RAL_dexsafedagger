This manuscript combines teacher–student action disagreement, a Bellman
success-value critic, and VLM-adjusted intervention thresholds for
dexterous policy distillation. The safety-oriented formulation and
ablations are relevant. However, the method provides no formal safety
guarantee, and the necessity of the VLM over conventional threshold
adaptation remains unclear. Real-world unseen-object results show only
60–66.7% success and 40% unsafe episodes. The limited repetitions,
critic calibration, safety definitions, prompt reproducibility, teacher
dependence, and sim-to-real limitations require substantial
strengthening.

Comments
1- The term “safety-critical” is too strong because the framework supplies
empirical intervention rather than formal constraint satisfaction.
State explicitly that safety violations remain possible.

2- Demonstrate that the VLM is necessary by comparing against fixed
thresholds, rule-based adaptation, Bayesian optimization, curriculum
schedules, grid search, and a small learned threshold network.

3- The Bellman critic predicts combined “safe-lift-success,” thereby
conflating task success with safety. Separate safety risk and task
value, and report calibration, false-negative rates, and reliability
diagrams.

4- Teacher intervention is assumed safe, yet teacher policies may collide
or fail. Quantify teacher unsafe rates and define how the framework
responds when teacher and critic disagree.

5- Provide exact VLM prompts, model version, decoding settings,
image-selection procedure, output parsing, update frequency, latency,
cost, and failure handling. Reproducibility may otherwise depend on an
external proprietary service.

6- Real-world unseen-object performance is weak: lift success falls to
60–66.7%, while unsafe episodes reach 40%. Claims of sufficient
deployment robustness should be moderated.

7- Only fifteen real-world episodes per reported object produce coarse
estimates. Increase repetitions and report confidence intervals and
statistical comparisons across all 22 objects.

8- Safety-event thresholds for harmful collision, object escape, palm
flipping, and workspace violation require physical justification.
Include contact-force measurements and severity-weighted safety
metrics.

9- Clarify whether VLM adaptation occurs only during simulation
distillation or during physical deployment. If absent at deployment,
the role of scene-level VLM reasoning should not be overstated.

10- Compare training samples, teacher queries, interventions, computation,
and final success across Vanilla DAgger, SafeDAgger, critic-based
intervention, and VLM-guided intervention using multiple random seeds.

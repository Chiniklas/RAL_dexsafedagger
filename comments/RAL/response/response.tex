\vspace{2em}
We sincerely thank the Editor and the reviewers for their careful evaluation
of our manuscript and for their constructive comments. Their feedback has
helped us identify where the contribution, safety claims, experimental
evidence, and implementation details require clearer presentation.

We are preparing a major revision of the manuscript. In the revised version,
we will:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
  \item clarify the specific contribution of VLM-guided threshold adaptation
  through comparisons with conventional fixed and adaptive alternatives;
  \item revise the safety terminology and explicitly delimit the empirical,
  task-specific scope of the reported safety results;
  \item strengthen the evaluation of the Bellman critic, teacher policies,
  real-world experiments, and resource usage; and
  \item provide the implementation details needed to reproduce the VLM-based
  threshold tuner and the intervention pipeline.
\end{enumerate}

Detailed responses to each comment are provided below. Reviewer comments are
reproduced in black, and our responses are shown in blue. Red bracketed text
marks details that will be completed after the corresponding revision or
experiment has been finalized. Changes in the revised manuscript will be
highlighted in blue.

\reviewerheading{Reviewer \#14}

We sincerely thank the reviewer for the careful assessment of our work and for
the constructive suggestions. Below are our point-by-point responses.

\reviewcomment{The current comparison between DexSafeDagger and
VLM-DexSafeDagger compares fixed thresholds with VLM-adapted thresholds and
therefore does not show whether the improvement comes from threshold adaptation,
language reasoning, or visual information. Please compare cases with and without
an LLM/VLM and with and without statistics and/or images.}

\response We have added ablations (g) and (h) to isolate the
contribution of the VLM to the adaptive intervention pipeline. We have also
expanded the discussion of Table~III in the Results section. The results
provide strong evidence that VLM-based intervention outperforms both LLM-based
intervention and performance-based curriculum adaptation. Semantic information
alone reduces the unsafe exploration rate, while combining visual and semantic
information enables more precise intervention, achieving the highest task
success rate and the lowest unsafe exploration rate. Although the
performance-based curriculum reduces unsafe exploration to some extent, it
sacrifices task success, suggesting that a simple rule-based method is
insufficient for guiding intervention policies in complex scenarios.

\reviewcomment{The related work on adaptive intervention should be expanded.
In particular, AIM, EnsembleDAgger, and HG-DAgger are relevant. Since adaptive
intervention can be achieved without a VLM, please clarify how
VLM-DexSafeDagger differs from these methods and whether the VLM provides an
advantage over simpler adaptive approaches.}

\response We have reformulated the Related Work section to more
clearly discuss adaptive intervention methods. In particular, AIM provides an
important foundation for our work, and we have added it to the reference list.
Compared with prior methods such as HG-DAgger and ThriftyDAgger, our proposed
pipeline does not require human intervention, enabling fully autonomous
intervention during online policy distillation. Regarding the advantage of VLMs
over simpler adaptive approaches, our results show that the VLM- and LLM-based
methods outperform the performance-based curriculum overall in terms of both
lift success and unsafe rate. We attribute this improvement to semantic
reasoning, further enhanced by visual context in the VLM, which enables more
effective adaptation in contact-rich, high-degree-of-freedom manipulation
tasks.

\response The details required to reproduce our method are provided
in the supplementary material. The exact VLM prompt and representative visual
input examples are included in Supplementary Section~B, while the number of
runs and random seeds is reported in Supplementary Section~A.3.

\reviewerheading{Reviewer \#22}

We sincerely thank the reviewer for the detailed and constructive comments.
Below are our point-by-point responses.

\reviewcomment{The term ``safety-critical'' is too strong because the framework
supplies empirical intervention rather than formal constraint satisfaction.
State explicitly that safety violations remain possible.}

\response We acknowledge that the term ``safety-critical'' is too strong for an
adaptive intervention pipeline such as ours. We have therefore revised the
title to ``VLM-DexSafeDagger: VLM-Guided Intervention Policy Towards Safer
Visuomotor Skill Distillation'' and removed or qualified the term elsewhere in
the manuscript. The revised text explicitly states that our method provides
empirical risk reduction under task-specific event definitions rather than a
formal safety guarantee, and that safety violations remain possible.

\reviewcomment{Demonstrate that the VLM is necessary by comparing against fixed
thresholds, rule-based adaptation, Bayesian optimization, curriculum schedules,
grid search, and a small learned threshold network.}

\response We have supplemented the ablation study with variants (g) and (h).
Together with variants (e) and (f), these experiments form a controlled ladder
of adaptive intervention strategies: fixed thresholds, a performance-based
curriculum, semantics-guided intervention-policy adaptation, and
visual-semantic-guided adaptive intervention. We have added a corresponding
discussion to the Results section. Table~III shows that adaptive intervention
reduces the unsafe rate relative to fixed thresholds. However, the
curriculum-based method sacrifices lift success, whereas incorporating semantic
information recovers task performance while further reducing unsafe
exploration. Overall, combining visual and semantic information outperforms the
other approaches, achieving the best balance between lift success and unsafe
rate.

\reviewcomment{The Bellman critic predicts combined ``safe-lift-success,''
thereby conflating task success with safety. Separate safety risk and task value,
and report calibration, false-negative rates, and reliability diagrams.}

\response The current joint target does not distinguish an unsafe outcome from
a safe episode that fails to complete the lift. In the revision, we
will \placeholder{state whether the critic is redesigned with separate safety
and task heads, or justify and retain the joint target}. We will report AUROC,
AUPRC, Brier score, expected calibration error, reliability diagrams, and the
false-negative rate at the deployed threshold on held-out trajectories.
\placeholder{Describe exactly how the revised critic enters the intervention
gate and insert the results.}

\reviewcomment{Teacher intervention is assumed safe, yet teacher policies may
collide or fail. Quantify teacher unsafe rates and define how the framework
responds when teacher and critic disagree.}

\response Teacher takeover is not guaranteed to be safe; it provides an
empirical tendency toward safer exploration rather than a formal guarantee. We
report the teacher policies' unsafe rates in Table~III of the main paper and,
in this revision, provide mean statistics across all tested objects. The
intervention gate follows a conservative OR rule: intervention is triggered if
either the teacher--student action disagreement exceeds its threshold or the
critic's predicted success falls below its threshold. Thus, when the two
signals disagree, the conservative outcome is to intervene. Comparing
ablations (c) and (e) shows that adding the critic-based branch substantially
reduces the unsafe exploration rate.

\reviewcomment{Provide exact VLM prompts, model version, decoding settings,
image-selection procedure, output parsing, update frequency, latency, cost, and
failure handling. Reproducibility may otherwise depend on an external
proprietary service.}

\response The details required to reproduce our method are provided in the
supplementary material. The exact VLM prompt, representative visual-input
examples, model and inference configuration, image-selection and
output-processing procedures, update frequency, token usage, and failure
handling are included in Supplementary Section~B. The number of runs and random
seeds is reported in Supplementary Section~A.3.

\reviewcomment{Real-world unseen-object performance is weak: lift success falls
to 60--66.7\%, while unsafe episodes reach 40\%. Claims of sufficient deployment
robustness should be moderated.}

\response We will remove the claim that the current results establish
sufficient robustness for deployment.

\reviewcomment{Only fifteen real-world episodes per reported object produce
coarse estimates. Increase repetitions and report confidence intervals and
statistical comparisons across all 22 objects.}

\response We have doubled the number of real-world evaluation episodes from 15
to 30 per object. We have also reported the complete results for all 22 tested
objects in Supplementary Section~C.

\reviewcomment{Safety-event thresholds for harmful collision, object escape,
palm flipping, and workspace violation require physical justification. Include
contact-force measurements and severity-weighted safety metrics.}

\response We have provided the exact definitions of all unsafe events in the
termination-conditions discussion in Supplementary Section~A.2. When multiple
unsafe conditions occur simultaneously during training, the event label is
assigned according to a clear severity-based priority: harmful collision,
object escape, palm flipped, and hand too far, in descending order.

\reviewcomment{Clarify whether VLM adaptation occurs only during simulation
distillation or during physical deployment. If absent at deployment, the role of
scene-level VLM reasoning should not be overstated.}

\response The VLM is queried only during simulation-based online distillation.
During physical deployment, the RGB student acts without the VLM, privileged
teacher, Bellman critic, or threshold adaptation. We will state this distinction
in the abstract, method, experimental setup, and limitations, and will describe
the VLM as a training-time adaptation mechanism rather than a deployment-time
safety monitor. \placeholder{Insert the final section locations.}

\reviewcomment{Compare training samples, teacher queries, interventions,
computation, and final success across Vanilla DAgger, SafeDAgger, critic-based
intervention, and VLM-guided intervention using multiple random seeds.}

\response The requested comparison across multiple random seeds is provided in
Supplementary Section~C.2.

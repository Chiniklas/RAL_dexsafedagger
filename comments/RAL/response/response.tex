\vspace{2em}
We sincerely thank the Editor and the reviewers for their careful evaluation
of our manuscript and for their constructive comments. Their feedback has
helped us identify where the contribution, safety claims, experimental
evidence, and implementation details require clearer presentation.

We are preparing a major revision of the manuscript. In the revised version,
we will:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
  \item clarify the specific contribution of VLM-guided threshold adaptation
  through comparisons with conventional fixed and adaptive alternatives;
  \item revise the safety terminology and explicitly delimit the empirical,
  task-specific scope of the reported safety results;
  \item strengthen the evaluation of the Bellman critic, teacher policies,
  real-world experiments, and resource usage; and
  \item provide the implementation details needed to reproduce the VLM-based
  threshold tuner and the intervention pipeline.
\end{enumerate}

Detailed responses to each comment are provided below. Reviewer comments are
reproduced in black, and our responses are shown in blue. Red bracketed text
marks details that will be completed after the corresponding revision or
experiment has been finalized. Changes in the revised manuscript will be
highlighted in blue.

\reviewerheading{the Associate Editor}

We thank the Associate Editor for summarizing the central issues raised during
review. We address each issue below and provide detailed point-by-point
responses to the reviewers in the following sections.

\reviewcomment{The experiments do not sufficiently separate the contribution
of the VLM from the effect of adaptive threshold tuning. Additional comparisons
and ablations are needed to clarify whether the VLM itself provides a benefit.}

\response We agree that the comparison between a fixed intervention policy and
VLM-guided adaptation does not, by itself, isolate the source of the
improvement. In the revision, we will add controlled comparisons covering fixed
threshold selection, conventional adaptive threshold rules, a learned threshold
controller, statistics-only language-model input, and statistics-plus-image VLM
input. All methods will use the same teacher, student, critic, training budget,
and evaluation protocol. We will report both task performance and intervention
cost so that safety improvements cannot be attributed only to increased teacher
control. \placeholder{Insert the finalized methods, principal quantitative
result, and revised table/figure locations.}

\reviewcomment{The framework provides empirical intervention rather than a
formal safety guarantee, and unsafe episodes remain in the real-world
experiments. The scope of the ``safety-critical'' claim should therefore be
clarified and better supported.}

\response We agree. The revised manuscript will explicitly state that the
framework does not guarantee constraint satisfaction and that safety violations
remain possible. We will replace overly strong uses of ``safety-critical'' with
terms such as ``safety-aware'' or ``empirically risk-reducing,'' and we will
limit our conclusions to the task-specific unsafe events measured in the
experiments. \placeholder{List the affected sections and the final revised
title, if applicable.}

\reviewcomment{The Bellman success-value critic, VLM reproducibility, number of
real-world trials, baseline coverage, and reporting for all tested objects need
to be strengthened.}

\response We agree and will expand both the evaluation and the implementation
description. The revision will include critic error and calibration analyses,
the complete VLM prompt and output-processing procedure, the number of random
seeds, matched baseline comparisons, uncertainty estimates, and aggregate as
well as per-object real-world results. \placeholder{Insert the completed
analyses and their manuscript or supplementary-material locations.}

We sincerely thank the reviewer for the careful assessment of our work and for

\reviewcomment{The current comparison between DexSafeDagger and
VLM-DexSafeDagger compares fixed thresholds with VLM-adapted thresholds and

\response We agree. We will add controlled ablations that separate adaptation,
language reasoning, and visual input. In particular, the revision will compare

rollout statistics only, a VLM receiving rollout statistics and selected
images, and a visual-control condition in which the image information is
removed or mismatched. These variants will be evaluated under matched training
and intervention budgets. \placeholder{Insert final ablation definitions,
results with uncertainty, and table/figure references.}

\reviewcomment{The related work on adaptive intervention should be expanded.
In particular, AIM, EnsembleDAgger, and HG-DAgger are relevant. Since adaptive

\response We agree and will expand the related-work discussion to include AIM,

human-gated intervention, learned intervention proxies, and our setting of
autonomous multi-object visuomotor distillation with a two-channel
disagreement/value gate. We will also ground the claimed benefit of the VLM in
the new adaptive-baseline experiments rather than in a conceptual distinction
alone. \placeholder{Insert citations and the revised section location.}

\reviewerheading{Reviewer \#22}

We sincerely thank the reviewer for the detailed and constructive comments.
Below are our point-by-point responses.

\reviewcomment{The term ``safety-critical'' is too strong because the framework
supplies empirical intervention rather than formal constraint satisfaction.
State explicitly that safety violations remain possible.}

\response We agree. The revised manuscript will state explicitly that the
method provides empirical risk reduction under task-specific event definitions,
not a formal safety guarantee, and that nonzero violation probability remains
in both simulation and physical deployment. We will revise the title, abstract,
main text, and conclusion to remove or qualify claims that exceed this scope.
\placeholder{Insert the final wording and section locations.}

\reviewcomment{Demonstrate that the VLM is necessary by comparing against fixed
thresholds, rule-based adaptation, Bayesian optimization, curriculum schedules,
grid search, and a small learned threshold network.}

\response We agree that stronger conventional baselines are required. We will
compare validation-selected fixed thresholds (using grid search and Bayesian
optimization) with adaptive curriculum, rule-based feedback, learned-network,
and VLM-guided controllers. The fixed methods will select one operating point,
whereas the adaptive methods will update the operating point during training.
All comparisons will use matched data and compute budgets where applicable and
will report task success, unsafe-event rate, intervention rate, teacher queries,
and training cost. \placeholder{Insert the final protocol, quantitative results,
and table/figure references.}

\reviewcomment{The Bellman critic predicts combined ``safe-lift-success,''
thereby conflating task success with safety. Separate safety risk and task value,
and report calibration, false-negative rates, and reliability diagrams.}

\response We agree that the current joint target does not distinguish an unsafe
outcome from a safe episode that fails to complete the lift. In the revision, we
will \placeholder{state whether the critic is redesigned with separate safety
and task heads, or justify and retain the joint target}. We will report AUROC,
AUPRC, Brier score, expected calibration error, reliability diagrams, and the
false-negative rate at the deployed threshold on held-out trajectories.
\placeholder{Describe exactly how the revised critic enters the intervention
gate and insert the results.}

\reviewcomment{Teacher intervention is assumed safe, yet teacher policies may
collide or fail. Quantify teacher unsafe rates and define how the framework
responds when teacher and critic disagree.}

\response We agree that teacher takeover is not itself a safety guarantee. We
will report success and unsafe-event rates for the teacher policies over the
complete evaluation set rather than only representative objects. We will also
specify how the controller handles disagreement between the critic's assessment
and the teacher action, including the fallback used when neither candidate
action satisfies the acceptance rule. \placeholder{Insert teacher statistics,
the fallback rule, and section/table references.}

\reviewcomment{Provide exact VLM prompts, model version, decoding settings,
image-selection procedure, output parsing, update frequency, latency, cost, and
failure handling. Reproducibility may otherwise depend on an external
proprietary service.}

\response We agree. The revised supplementary material will include all of the
requested information, together with the permitted threshold range, smoothing
rule, invalid-output handling, and deterministic fallback. We will archive the
raw VLM outputs and threshold trajectories where allowed so that the adaptation
process can be audited independently of the external service. In the reported
run, all ten scheduled VLM calls completed successfully; we encountered no
request timeout, API failure, or malformed VLM output. The implementation
details and recorded token usage are provided in
Appendix~\ref{app:vlm_inference_details}.

\response We agree. We will remove the claim that the current results establish
sufficient robustness for deployment. Instead, we will present the physical
experiments as an initial transfer evaluation and identify the unseen-object
results as evidence of a remaining sim-to-real and contact-generalization gap.
\placeholder{Insert the revised discussion and limitation-section locations.}

\reviewcomment{Only fifteen real-world episodes per reported object produce
coarse estimates. Increase repetitions and report confidence intervals and

\response We agree that the uncertainty of the current estimates must be made

\reviewcomment{Safety-event thresholds for harmful collision, object escape,
palm flipping, and workspace violation require physical justification. Include
contact-force measurements and severity-weighted safety metrics.}

\response We agree that each event definition requires a physical and sensing
rationale. The revision will list the numerical threshold, sensor or simulator
signal, units, and justification for every event. In addition to binary episode
incidence, we will report \placeholder{the available force, impulse, motor-current,
penetration, or other severity proxy}. If calibrated force measurements are not

\reviewcomment{Clarify whether VLM adaptation occurs only during simulation
distillation or during physical deployment. If absent at deployment, the role of
scene-level VLM reasoning should not be overstated.}

\response The VLM is queried only during simulation-based online distillation.
During physical deployment, the RGB student acts without the VLM, privileged
teacher, Bellman critic, or threshold adaptation. We will state this distinction
in the abstract, method, experimental setup, and limitations, and will describe
the VLM as a training-time adaptation mechanism rather than a deployment-time
safety monitor. \placeholder{Insert the final section locations.}

\reviewcomment{Compare training samples, teacher queries, interventions,
computation, and final success across Vanilla DAgger, SafeDAgger, critic-based
intervention, and VLM-guided intervention using multiple random seeds.}

\response We agree. We will add a unified comparison using the same training
horizon and paired random seeds. For each method, we will report environment
steps, student updates, teacher labels, teacher-controlled steps, intervention
rate, compute time, VLM calls where applicable, final lift success, final unsafe
rate, and unsafe-rate area under the training curve. \placeholder{Insert the
number of seeds, quantitative results, and table/figure references.}

% Dexterous robotic hands, which mimic the anthropomorphic structure of
% the human hand, typically possess a large number of degrees of freedom
% (DoFs). This high level of dexterity makes them well suited for
% complex and contact-rich tasks such as grasping (e.g., pinching,
% tripod, and power grasps) and in-hand manipulation (e.g., reorientation
% and twisting). However, this same dexterity also introduces mechanical
% delicacy and control complexity, making dexterous hands among the most
% fragile components in robotic systems, particularly in highly dynamic
% platforms such as humanoid robots. 

Dexterous robotic hands possess many degrees of freedom, offering substantial
potential for complex manipulation tasks while exhibiting a significant
sim-to-real gap due to complex contact dynamics~\cite{brahmbhatt2019contactgrasp,chen2022system,andrychowicz2020learning,luo2025precise}.
Reinforcement learning (RL)-based approaches~\cite{rajeswaran2017learning,andrychowicz2020learning,akkaya2019solving,petrenko2023dexpbt}
have recently made significant progress in performing complex dexterous
manipulation tasks in simulation, enabled by increasingly accurate contact-dynamics
modeling and advances in high-dimensional control.

Existing RL frameworks acquire complex skills primarily by optimizing task
success rates. For sim-to-real transfer, visuomotor student-policy distillation
is commonly employed to transfer simulated skills into real-world behaviors.
However, without explicit modeling of safety-related constraints during
distillation, failure modes such as severe collisions between the hand and the
environment, unintended object ejection due to excessive contact forces, and
loss of stable contact posture~\cite{liu2023safe,yu2022dexterous} can be passed
from imperfect teachers to students.

Existing safety-aware distillation approaches~\cite{}, such as SafeDAgger~\cite{}, have primarily focused on autonomous driving scenarios and have not been systematically studied in the context of dexterous manipulation.

During distillation training, student policies imitate teacher behavior through
repeated demonstrations~\cite{singh2024dextrah}. In many
cases, offline approaches are time-consuming and data-inefficient. By
parallelizing data collection and policy training, interactive imitation
learning, represented by DAgger-based algorithms~\cite{ross2011reduction},
provides a natural backbone for online distillation pipelines. However, these
approaches (e.g., SafeDAgger~\cite{zhang2016query}) primarily address
performance degradation caused by distribution shift through expert
intervention rather than explicitly modeling task-specific physical safety
constraints during student-policy learning. More precisely, imitation errors
can drive the student beyond the teacher's state--action distribution into
underrepresented or high-risk states, causing severe collisions or unstable
hand configurations.
Therefore, introducing explicit safety mechanisms into the distillation
process---so that high-risk exploration can be avoided without sacrificing
learning efficiency---remains an important and largely underexplored problem.

The contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We propose \methodname, a VLM-guided,
    safety-sensitive framework for
    distilling RL-acquired teacher policies into a single RGB-based student
    policy. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first attempt in dexterous manipulation to incorporate
    a mixed intervention policy combining teacher--student action disagreement
    with a Bellman success-value critic, while the VLM reasons about scene-level safety from rollout statistics and
    representative visual observations to regulate the intervention gate.

    \item We empirically demonstrate that \methodname, which combines
    disagreement, success-value prediction, and VLM-guided intervention policy adaptation,
    substantially reduces unsafe exploration compared with Vanilla DAgger,
    achieving an approximately \textbf{80\%} relative reduction in the
    unsafe-episode rate during early training. It also reduces the average
    unsafe-episode rate by \textbf{7.5 percentage points} relative to
    disagreement-only SafeDAgger~\cite{zhang2016query}. Ablation results further demonstrate the
    effectiveness of VLM-guided adaptation under randomized visual conditions,
    with safety evaluated using four task-specific unsafe-event categories:
    \methodname consistently achieves lower unsafe-episode
    rates than \dexsafedagger, with an average reduction of
    \textbf{2.6 percentage points}.
\end{itemize}
